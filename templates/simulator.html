The numbers behind it

January– . "Average price" is total value ÷ units, so it mixes cheap and premium models: a country that ships high-end phones shows a higher average. That's why the chart compares the same $100 of goods instead.

How it's calculated

Landed cost of $100 of goods = $100 + freight and insurance + tariff. Freight is each country's actual freight and insurance charges as a share of the goods' value this year. The tariff is the effective rate charged in the latest month (duty ÷ value), which already reflects exemptions and trade-agreement treatment.

What it leaves out

Factory price differences, quality, lead times, minimum orders, supplier reliability, US inland shipping, and the cost of moving production. A real sourcing decision needs those too; this shows the part that customs data can answer.

Freight is a bigger share for cheap, bulky goods, so a country selling cheaper versions shows higher freight per $100. For Mexico and Canada, freight is counted only to the US border, so trucking costs look small.

Data

US Census Bureau international trade data (HTS 10-digit products, imports for consumption, calculated duty, charges), downloaded . Code and method on GitHub.
